SLIDE 2: PROBLEM
A one-person SaaS team loses a fifth of its engineering week to glue work.
Indie SaaS founders run their business across four or five tools that do not talk
to each other: GitHub for code, Linear for issues, Stripe for billing, Vercel for
deploys. Shipping one feature means context-switching between all of them and
hand-wiring the connections.

In our survey of 210 solo and two-person SaaS teams, respondents put integration
and tool-switching at 12-20 hours a week.

SLIDE 3: SOLUTION
One command replaces four dashboards.
Acmecorp is a single CLI and AI agent that spans the stack. Describe the change in
plain language; Acmecorp opens the branch, files the issue, wires the Stripe
product, and ships the preview deploy.

    $ acme ship "add annual billing at 20% off"

One command, four systems updated, one PR to review.

SLIDE 5: WHY NOW
Agents only became reliable enough for multi-tool writes in the last 18 months.
Cross-tool automation has been attempted before and failed on two things that
changed recently: models could not be trusted to take destructive actions across
systems, and the four platforms did not expose the write APIs required. Both are
now true. GitHub, Linear, Stripe and Vercel all shipped write-capable APIs in the
last two years, and tool-use reliability crossed the threshold where a founder
will let an agent open a PR unattended.

The window is open and it is not permanently open — the platforms could close it.

SLIDE 6: PRODUCT
The agent shows its work before it touches anything.
- CLI with an AI agent that plans multi-tool changes and shows a diff first
- Bidirectional sync across GitHub, Linear, Stripe and Vercel
- Rollback across all four when a deploy is reverted
- Self-hosted runner for teams that will not send code to a third party
